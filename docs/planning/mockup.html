Driver-based budgeting and forecasting on Singlepane

What the current data model can carry, where the forecast engine should live, how it stays in step with actuals, and three screens that replace ProfitSage's seven tabs. The screens are live mockups: edit a driver and the numbers move.

What exists today

Left: Singlepane, read from the sp-core repo and the live SPH database. Right: ProfitSage, from clicking through its forecast input screens. Nothing was edited there.

Singlepane

  • One fact table. cleaned.account_values holds hotel code × operator GL account × month × version. 88M rows, 45 GB, not partitioned. The key is (code, time, version, account_id). No scenario or source column, and loads overwrite with no history.
  • Versions are text. actual, budget, budget1–12, forecast1–12 (the reforecast after month N closes, with months 1..N copied from actuals), forecastN.M (weekly revision), proforma variants. The valid list and the "latest forecast" order are hard-coded in about six functions, with copies on legacy.
  • One global hierarchy. account_map is global and mapping_override is per hotel. Rollups are computed on read. Today that goes through account_map_cache; PR 114's single-pass cube drops it and resolves each account's line from the override or the map, then its ancestors from account_ancestor_map. There is no alternate rollup per company.
  • Operator charts are far bigger than any hotel's. mgr_chart_of_accounts holds 52,031 accounts for 41 management companies, with a department on most and parent_account empty. One operator has about 20,800 mapped accounts, and four of its hotels each used 430 to 1,400 in the last twelve months.
  • Statistics ride in the same table under stat accounts. Most operators have their own stat GLs. For stat ledgers and ProfitSword-style exports the loader invents one by appending -STAT or -STAT-ACCT to the amount GL: about 2,400 accounts today, mostly two operators. Rooms available has its own side table.
  • The USALI reference already knows each line's natural driver. reference.usali has a default metric and denominator for nearly every line: POR (1,940 lines), % of S&W (1,556), per F&B guest (1,011), % of department revenue, ADR on the matching rooms-sold line, plus department and category.
  • Daily data is deep for pace, thin for the P&L. On-the-books by segment by stay date is 144M rows from Demand360, IDeaS, Delphi and PMS feeds. account_values_daily is 150K rows from five feeds.
  • IDeaS forecasts already land. raw.ideas_busview has occupancy forecast, forecasted room revenue and ADR forecast by business view by day. Nothing reads those columns yet.
  • Labor is hours only, by position by day from Hotel Effectiveness. Wages are GL dollars.
  • No transaction table. A few raw landings are one row per posting (IHG GL daily, Opera TB, Workday TB) and are collapsed on load.
  • No drivers, assumptions, seasonality or scenarios for hotel P&Ls anywhere in the database.
  • Context ProfitSage does not have: abstracted contracts, loans and rate curves, STR, pace, labor standards, GM commentary through Scout, approval workflows.

ProfitSage

  • One formula per account. Driver × Ratio = Stat, then Stat × Rate = Amount. Expenses stop at Driver × Ratio. Labor is Driver × Ratio = hours, × wage rate.
  • One driver per account, assigned elsewhere. On the input grid the driver's name appears only in a hover tooltip.
  • Seven tabs for the same numbers (Month, Day, Days, By Days, By Periods, 12 Months, Plan), times three account types, times a department picker inside a settings dialog.
  • Plan is a separate tab. Named items with fixed monthly amounts and an "Offset to Actuals" row. No drivers inside a plan, and an account is either planned or driven.
  • Spreading is "Set Copy Range". No seasonality profiles on the input screens.
  • Datasets: Actuals, Actuals/Defs, Act+Cmt/Defs+Tents, Primary Forecast, Budget, Secondary Budget, Flex and named snapshots, with up to two comparisons picked in the same dialog.
  • Drill-down opens a new tab listing transactions: date, type, description, stat, rate, amount, vendor, invoice or PO.
  • Each view change took 5 to 10 seconds.
  • Commentary is one free-text box per account, 4,096 characters.
Read-across. Keep the stat × rate decomposition, daily revenue with monthly expenses, dataset comparisons, drill to transactions, group definites and tentatives layered on actuals, and flex. Beat it on one grid instead of seven, drivers that are visible and editable in place, several dated components per line, templates that stay linked to the operator standard, speed, and an agent that maintains the drivers.
Verdict on the existing structure. Build on it. The engine is a new schema that plans at the same grain the fact table already uses (hotel × operator GL account × period), reads actuals and statistics from it, and publishes finished forecasts back into it under the version names every screen, Excel function and MCP tool already understands.

Rules the model follows

  • Plan at the grain already stored. Lines key on the same account string as account_values, so publishing is a plain insert and USALI rollups come from the existing map.
  • A line is a sum of components. Multi-driver lines and ProfitSage "Plans" are the same structure: one component per item, each with its own method and dates. "New laundry pricing from June 1" ends one component and starts another.
  • Defaults with sparse overrides. A rate has one value and optional month overrides. Change the default and every month that was not overridden follows. No copy range.
  • Scenario is a real column here. Inputs and results both carry it. Locking copies the inputs (a few thousand rows) and freezes the results, so a locked forecast can always explain itself.
  • One write path. The grid, the MCP tools and the agent all call the same patch function. Nobody writes result cells.
  • Transactions attach to components by vendor and rule. Actual versus plan then works per item, which replaces "Offset to Actuals" and gives the agent something to reason about.

Component methods

  • Fixed: an amount per period, optional escalator. Uniform cleaning, $640 a month.
  • Per unit: driver × rate. Guest supplies, $4.85 per occupied room.
  • Headcount: FTEs × hours × wage, or FTEs × salary. One housekeeping supervisor. Hours are stored as a statistic, so productivity comes free.
  • Stat chain: driver × ratio = stat, stat × rate = amount. Occupied rooms × 0.34 capture = breakfast covers, × $24.50 average check. The covers are stored as a stat that other lines can use.
  • Percent of: a share of another line or set. Payroll taxes, 31.5% of rooms wages.
  • Flex: base × (fixed % + variable % × driver ÷ base driver). Utilities, 70% fixed.
  • Schedule: explicit amounts by month. Window cleaning in April and October.
  • Feed: the value arrives from a source. Rooms and rate by segment by day from the RMS.
  • History seed: derive a ratio from actuals (trailing twelve, same month last year) and store it as an ordinary parameter. This is how a hotel gets a full model on day one.

Sets, grouping and stat accounts

Three things the engine needs before operator hierarchies exist. All three can come from the mapping to Singlepane USALIs that every GL already has. Counts are from today's account_map and reference.usali.

Sets of GLs as a driver

  • A set is a USALI node, not a list. "Rooms salaries and wages" is every enabled GL in this hotel whose mapping rolls up to Total Salaries Wages & Bonuses - 100. Members are resolved the way PR 114's cube resolves accounts: the hotel's mapping_override if it has one for the account, otherwise account_map, then ancestors from account_ancestor_map. Nothing is cached, so new GLs join on their own and a mapping change takes effect at the next recalculation.
  • The defaults exist. Each USALI line's default denominator names its node: a payroll-related GL defaults to a percent of its department's S&W node, a supplies GL to POR on rooms occupied.
  • Templates state sets relative to the line: "S&W in my department". One operator rule then covers Rooms, each F&B outlet and every undistributed department.
  • Includes and excludes handle an operator whose subtotal differs from the USALI node (bonuses out, contract labor in). This is the stand-in for operator hierarchies on day one.
  • The engine sums the members' forecast values in the scenario being calculated. A set that contains the line it drives is rejected when saved.

Grouping the workspace

  • Use the USALI mapping. Each GL's leaf gives a department (Rooms, F&B and its outlet, other operated, undistributed) and a category (salaries wages and bonuses, payroll-related, cost of sales, other expenses, the revenue types, the statistic types).
  • The group rows already exist in the hierarchy per department: Total Salaries Wages & Bonuses, Total Payroll-Related Expenses, Total Labor Costs & Related Expenses, Total Other Expenses.
  • Resolve leaf, department and category when the workspace loads, with the same lookup the cube uses. Storing them on the line would be another cache to keep in step.
  • Edge cases. Unmapped GLs sit in an Unmapped group and block a lock. A GL split across USALIs, about 1% of accounts, groups under its largest share.

Stat accounts for stat chains

  • Each amount line that produces a statistic has a paired stat account. A stat chain or labor component writes the statistic there and the dollars to the amount GL in the same recalculation. Several amount lines can share one stat account; the engine sums them. A stat account is either fed this way or forecast on its own, never both.
  • Suffix operators pair mechanically: strip -STAT or -STAT-ACCT. On today's mapping that pairs wages with hours worked (1,164 accounts), room revenue with rooms sold (238) and food revenue with guests (130).
  • Operators with their own stat GLs need one pass per chart. The USALI narrows the candidates but rarely to one: on one large operator chart, 75 wage GLs had exactly one candidate hours GL and 2,008 had several. Propose pairs by department segment and description inside the USALI candidates, and have a person approve them, as account mapping proposals work today.
  • No stat GL at all: create <amount GL>-STAT with the loader's convention, mapped to the paired stat USALI, so forecast hours and covers have a home.
  • A USALI-level pair table seeds all of it. 931 of 973 S&W lines have an Hours Worked twin by name, and revenue lines already name their rooms-sold or guest line as the default denominator.

Chart of accounts

An operator's chart is far larger than what one hotel uses, and a hotel can start forecasting a GL before it has any actuals. So which accounts are in a hotel's forecast and budget is an explicit setting that can be reviewed and edited. The fourth mockup shows the hotel view.

Operator chart

  • The universe is the operator's full chart: mgr_chart_of_accounts plus any account already in account_map for that operator. One screen to review and edit it: description, department, mapped USALI, paired stat account, default driver, and whether the account is on by default at the operator's hotels.
  • An account can be retired for the whole operator. A retired account cannot be enabled at a hotel.
  • A new account needs a mapping before it can be enabled. With the cache gone there is no trigger that places an unmapped account anywhere, so this screen is where the mapping is guaranteed, through the existing mapping proposal flow.

Hotel chart

  • Each hotel has its own status per account: enabled or off, with an enabled-from and a disabled-from month.
  • First setup proposes the list: on for accounts with actuals in the last 12 to 24 months that map to a P&L or statistic line, off for the rest.
  • Turning on an account with no history creates its line from the operator standard's driver, or from the USALI default metric, and it appears in the workspace from its enabled-from month. This is the hotel that starts using a new GL in the forecast or budget.
  • Turning an account off ends its components at that month. History and locked scenarios keep it.
  • Actuals that post to an account that is off go to a needs-attention list, and the agent proposes enabling it.
  • Sets count actuals for every member account and forecast values for the enabled ones.
Labor. Stays at GL level, as in ProfitSage, but a wage line is built from components: a supervisor at 1 FTE, plus room attendants at 0.5 hours per occupied room and $20 an hour. Positions, schedules and payroll feeds are out of v1.
Hierarchies. The single USALI hierarchy is enough to start because lines live at GL level and rollups are a read concern. Operator presentations come later as a second tree on mgr_chart_of_accounts.parent_account, a column that exists but is not populated yet. The one place the engine touches a hierarchy is subtotal drivers ("% of rooms wages"), so those point at a stored set of lines that either tree can generate.

Engine and stack

Postgres is sufficient, and the calculation should run inside it. A hotel forecast is a small problem repeated many times. It only gets large in aggregate, and the aggregate splits cleanly by hotel.

Calculate in SQL, by dependency level

The driver vocabulary is closed (nine methods), so the engine is a handful of set-based statements run once per dependency level: statistics, then lines driven by statistics, then subtotals, then lines that are a percent of subtotals. Each line stores its level; cycles are rejected when a driver is saved.

One function, planning.apply_patch(), validates the change, writes the inputs, recalculates the dirty slice and returns the changed cells and headline KPIs. A full hotel recalculation should be well under a second and an edit a few milliseconds of database time. The grid updates the edited row locally and reconciles when the call returns.

The alternative is a TypeScript engine shared by browser and server. It gives instant previews of everything but puts the numbers in two places and adds a worker tier. I would only go there if customers need free-form formulas.

Live everywhere: write through to the fact table

The working forecast is a real version in account_values (and account_values_daily for daily lines), called working. Each recalculation upserts only the cells whose value changed, in the same transaction as planning.result. Because the cube computes rollups on read, dashboards, Excel and MCP show the new number on their next query.

For a hotel on the planner, latest_forecast resolves to working. Locks are copied to forecastN, forecastN.M and budget exactly as file loads are today, so forecast accuracy and as-of-date logic keep comparing against frozen versions.

The other route is to have the cube read planning.result directly. I would avoid it: it puts a second source inside the most performance-sensitive function in the database.

Version registry, in the first release

Adding working means touching every function that hard-codes the version list. Do that once, as a table: version key, family (actual, budget, forecast, proforma), author (operator or owner), kind (official, weekly, working), month and revision, rank for "latest", and whether it bypasses the validation gate. Seed it with today's ninety-odd values and prove parity before anything else changes.

The same table lets an owner's forecast sit beside the operator's: owner_working, owner_forecast3, owner_budget, visible only to the authoring company. The owner screens can wait; the keys are expensive to retrofit.

Custom scenarios and one version list

  • Any scenario can be saved under a name, from the working forecast or a what-if, and kept live or locked. Official locks keep the forecastN and forecastN.M names. Everything else gets its own registry row: key, label, company, hotels, kind, author, locked at.
  • Every version dropdown reads one function. For the hotels in view it returns the system versions that have data plus the custom scenarios the caller's company can see. The app, Excel and MCP all use it, so nothing lists versions by hand.
  • Five custom scenarios per hotel, for now. Each is written to the fact table like working and shows everywhere, at about 36,000 rows per hotel. Archiving one frees its slot and removes its fact rows; it stays viewable in the planner. What-ifs do not count: they are temporary and stay in the planner.
  • Fork: follows its source. Made from the working forecast or any other scenario. It stores only its own changes, each either a replacement ("group rooms are 1,200 in July") or an adjustment to the source's value ("occupied rooms 8% lower, June to September"), and reads everything else from the source each time it recalculates.
  • A fork follows actuals as months close, the top line feed where it left the top line alone, every driver it did not change, newly enabled accounts, and all the lines calculated from those. It does not follow a driver it replaced: that stays as set even if the source's moves, and the fork's change list flags it. Forks recalculate from the queue after their source changes, a few seconds behind it.
  • Copy: independent. A full copy of the drivers that no longer follows its source. It still takes actuals as months close. Feed-driven lines such as an RMS top line are frozen at their values when copied, so a copy moves only with actuals and its own edits.
  • Snapshot: fully locked. A frozen copy of drivers and results that does not change, even when actuals arrive. Official locks are snapshots.
  • How they relate. Whether a scenario takes actuals is its own property, so a fork of a snapshot is live. A fork can be detached into a copy, and anything can be snapshotted. A snapshot cannot be reopened, only forked or copied. Archiving a scenario turns its forks into copies.
  • Scenarios are defined per company, with a list of hotels, so the same scenario rolls up across them in a portfolio view. A hotel that is not in the scenario contributes its working forecast to that rollup, which needs a fallback rule in the version resolver.
  • Latest forecast, forecast accuracy and as-of logic ignore custom scenarios.

Built on the single-pass cube (PR 114)

  • No use of account_map_cache anywhere in the planner. Sets, grouping and write-through read account_map, mapping_override and account_ancestor_map directly, as the new cube does.
  • One resolver. Lift PR 114's account-to-line lookup into a shared function, so the planner and the cube cannot disagree about where an account maps.
  • A mapping change queues a recalculation. Without the cache the cube reflects a remap at once, but percent-of-set lines hold calculated values until the planner recalculates.
  • Registry after the switch. PR 114 removes get_financial_data_ib and _lf and moves latest-forecast handling into get_financial_data_cells. Build the registry and working on those functions after its switch and cleanup, so nothing is converted twice.
  • Every cube reader is coming off the cache, the daily cube included, and the planner does not wait for that. Its monthly and daily write-through only add rows to the fact tables; whichever cube reads them resolves the mapping itself.
  • A live version is cheap for the new cube. It adds months per hotel account before mapping, so working is one more version of facts, and the PR measures mapping at about 6 microseconds an account per call.

Live versus locked

  • Working forecast. One per hotel, live everywhere as the working version. Closed months are actuals. Open months are calculated. It moves when drivers, actuals or feeds move.
  • Open month. Daily lines are actual to date plus forecast for the remaining days. Monthly expense lines keep the forecast until close and show posted-to-date beside it.
  • Official lock. An immutable copy of inputs and results. Monthly and weekly locks keep the forecastN and forecastN.M names.
  • Custom scenario. Any name, defined per company, made from the working forecast or any other scenario as a fork (follows its source), a copy (independent, still takes actuals) or a snapshot (fully locked). Up to five per hotel. It appears in every version dropdown.
  • Budget. The same model on a different scenario. No approval workflow in v1: a permitted user locks it.
  • What-if. An unpublished branch of any scenario; the agent previews proposals in one. Saving it under a name makes it a custom scenario.
  • Flex. The budget's drivers recalculated on actual statistics. With a driver model this costs nothing extra.

What needs care

  • Partition planning.result and ledger.transaction from the first migration (by hotel hash and by posting month). account_values at 45 GB unpartitioned is the cautionary case.
  • Thin old snapshots. Keep monthly line totals; drop component and daily detail after a set age.
  • Write only changed cells. account_values is 45 GB and unpartitioned. An edit should touch hundreds of rows there and a nightly feed refresh thousands, never the whole scenario.
  • Triggers. The provisional-validation trigger fires on every insert statement and must skip versions the registry marks as bypassing validation. The cache-maintenance trigger goes away with the cache, so a new account needs a mapping before its first write or the cube will not place it.
  • First appearance lags. A hotel's first working rows for a period become "latest" after the next five-minute status rebuild. Later edits to existing rows show immediately. PR 112's incremental status job would shorten the first lag.
  • Legacy receives the working version in the nightly copy and ignores it until its own version lists learn the key.
  • Concurrency. Row versions on components so two people editing one hotel do not overwrite each other; departments edit their own lines.

Stack. Postgres for definitions, calculation and results. Hasura exposes the patch and read functions, as it does for the rest of the app. The queue worker is pg_cron draining a table with skip-locked. Lambdas are for the accounting connectors and the agent only, following the existing event-trigger pattern. The grid is a virtualized table in the existing UI behind a module entitlement.

Actuals and transactions

Two steps. First, GL-detail files through the report-type pipeline you already run, landing in ledger.transaction instead of being collapsed. Second, API connectors where the system has a good API. The trial balance stays as the control total: transactions summed by account and month must tie to it, and that check feeds the existing validation status.

All three v1 systems start on files. That also covers the case where the owner is the customer and the operator runs the books. The M3, Intacct and Duetto rows reflect a quick check of public material on 18 November; confirm against a customer tenant.

AI-native, in two directions

You tell it. "Update the forecast for the new laundry pricing from June 1." The agent finds the line and component, reads the contract if it is in Contracts, drafts a patch, shows the dollar impact in a what-if, and applies it when approved.

It tells you. Cheap SQL detectors run after each ledger sync and month close. An LLM step turns each finding into an explanation, a proposed patch and, when the right answer depends on something only a person knows, a question.

Tools (MCP and in-app, same functions as the grid)

  • explain_line: components, drivers, history, recent transactions
  • preview_patch: impact by month and on GOP, in a what-if
  • apply_patch: writes the change with reason and evidence
  • list_suggestions, answer_suggestion
  • compare_scenarios: a bridge between two forecasts by driver
  • lock_scenario

Detectors

  • A recurring vendor charge steps up or down and stays there
  • A new vendor appears twice on the same account
  • An expected recurring invoice is missing near close
  • Actual cost per unit drifts from the driver for three months
  • A one-off spike that should not enter the run rate
  • An annual item lands in a different month than planned
  • Posted-to-date already exceeds the month's forecast

Policy, by account and threshold

  • Off. The agent reports and never proposes. A sensible default for wages.
  • Ask. A question to the line's owner through Scout's commentary thread; the answer becomes the patch and the comment.
  • Suggest. Queue for approval with evidence and impact.
  • Autopilot. Apply when the change is under the account's limits (dollars a month, percent, kind of change), report afterward, one-click undo.

Policy rows sit on the operator template, hotel, department or account, and the most specific wins. An automatic change is attributed to the policy and to the person who set it; the line's owner stays accountable. Locked scenarios are never touched.

Better drivers than they have today

  • History seed. Every line gets a starting driver from its own actuals, so a hotel has a full model on day one.
  • Driver discovery. For each account, fit candidate drivers (occupied rooms, arrivals, guests, covers, revenue, days) against 24 to 36 months. The intercept is the fixed part and the slope the variable rate. Recurring vendors in the ledger become named components.
  • Driver fit check. On every line, a drawer tab applies today's drivers to the actual volumes of the last 12 months and compares the result with what posted: average error, bias and the worst month. Those figures are calculated by the engine, the same calculation as flex, so they come out the same every time. Where transactions are matched to components, the check can run per component.
  • Fit suggestions are the agent's. The engine screens every line cheaply and passes the agent the ones that fit badly, with candidate fits, the ledger and any contract. The agent decides what the miss is (a fixed base, a price step, a one-time invoice to leave out, a different driver), writes the proposal, and the engine scores it on the same 12 months before it is shown. It lands in the same inbox, under the same account policy, as every other suggestion.
  • ProfitSword import. Where a customer can export it, their driver and ratio per account loads as the baseline, and the agent lists where the fit says a different driver or a fixed and variable split would forecast better.
Where Singlepane starts ahead. Years of GL history build a first driver set without anyone typing a ratio. Pace seeds the top line. Hotel Effectiveness standards can seed labor ratios. Later, and not in v1: contract terms seeding fixed components and escalators, and loans with the rate curve forecasting debt service.

Build order for v1

  • Spike. Rebuild one real hotel's current operator forecast from seeded drivers and see how close the engine gets. In parallel, land GL detail for one operator.
  • Version registry. After PR 114's switch and cleanup: replace the hard-coded lists with a table, prove parity, add working.
  • Chart of accounts. Operator chart review, per-hotel enable and deactivate, stat pairing.
  • Monthly expenses and labor. Engine, forecast workspace, components including headcount, operator templates, write-through, locks to forecastN.
  • Top line. Monthly entry first, then the daily grid with IDeaS and Duetto, and F&B stat chains.
  • Transactions. GL-detail files for M3, Sage and PeopleSoft, drill-through, component matching.
  • Agent. Chat edits and driver discovery first, then suggestions, then autopilot under account policies.
  • Not in v1. Approvals, Delphi definites and tentatives, below-GOP lines seeded from contracts and loans, shared services and allocations, multi-currency, operator hierarchies, API connectors beyond Intacct, owner screens.

Still open

  1. Should "latest forecast" mean the live working forecast everywhere for a hotel on the planner? I have assumed yes, which matches ProfitSword's Primary Forecast. The alternative is the last locked forecast, with working as a version people choose.
  2. Is an owner's forecast private to the owner? I have assumed the operator cannot see owner versions and the owner sees the operator's as today.
  3. Do department heads enter their own sections in v1, with edit rights by department, or does one finance user per hotel own the whole forecast?
  4. Can a friendly customer export their ProfitSword driver setup? That decides whether the import is real or theoretical.
  5. Who at M3 and at Duetto can answer the outbound-data question? Folio and Trintech already have M3 connections, so the access exists.
  6. Which hotel is the spike hotel? Ideally one where you receive the operator's ProfitSword forecast and have several years of GL history, so the engine's output can be compared line by line.
  7. In a copy, should feed-driven lines such as an RMS top line freeze at their values when copied? I have assumed yes, so a copy moves only with actuals and its own edits.
  8. Who approves stat pairings for an operator with its own stat GLs: Singlepane ops at onboarding, or the operator's finance team?
  9. Does v1 need to be in customers' hands for the 2028 budget season? Operators start budgets in late summer, which would put the engine, workspace and monthly top line ahead of everything else.
